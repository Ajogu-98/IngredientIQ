Optical Brighteners

The reason your whites 'glow' in sunlight. They don't clean anything — they coat fabric with a fluorescent dye that stays there.

← Back !Use With Caution
Quick answerOptical brighteners (also called fluorescent whitening agents) are dyes that absorb ultraviolet light and re-emit it as visible blue, making fabric look whiter and brighter. They work by staying on the cloth after washing, which means they're in constant contact with skin. They're a minor contact allergen, they can cause photosensitivity in a few people, and most are poorly biodegradable and toxic to aquatic life. Not dangerous for most people; worth avoiding for babies, eczema, and anyone who wants their laundry actually clean rather than optically clean.

What is Optical Brighteners?

Optical brighteners are synthetic dyes — most based on stilbene, coumarin, or biphenyl structures — that fluoresce. They absorb invisible UV light and emit it as blue visible light, which cancels the yellowish tinge that white fabric develops with age and washing.

They're designed to bind to cotton and other fibres and resist rinsing. Every wash tops up the coating. Under a blacklight, brightener-treated clothing glows; that's the dye you're wearing.

What does it do in a product?

Nothing in the way of cleaning. Brighteners don't remove stains or dirt; they mask the dull appearance of fabric so it looks cleaner than it is. Detergent makers include them because consumers judge cleaning by whiteness.

They're also in some paper, plastics, and cosmetics for the same reason, and in 'brightening' laundry boosters.

Is Optical Brighteners safe?

Acute toxicity is low, and skin absorption of the common brighteners is minimal. The concerns are modest and chronic. Because the dye stays on fabric, skin — especially a baby's, or eczema-prone skin — is in continuous contact with it. Allergic contact dermatitis to optical brighteners is documented but uncommon; it's more often found in people already reacting to fragrances or other laundry residues.

Some brighteners are photosensitisers: under UV light they can generate reactive species on the skin. Reports of photoallergic reactions exist but are rare.

The more consistent problem is environmental. Stilbene brighteners are poorly biodegradable, are toxic to fish and aquatic invertebrates, and are among the most frequently detected synthetic compounds in surface water downstream of wastewater plants. The EU Ecolabel and several eco-certification schemes exclude them for this reason.

They are not carcinogenic by current assessments and are not restricted for health reasons in any major jurisdiction.

Who should be careful

  • Babies and young children — brightener-free 'baby' detergents exist for this reason
  • Anyone with eczema, sensitive skin, or unexplained rashes in areas covered by clothing (waistband, underarm, collar)
  • People with photosensitivity conditions or on photosensitising medication
  • Households on septic systems or in areas with sensitive waterways
  • Anyone who wants to see when clothes are actually clean — brighteners hide the difference

Where you'll find it

  • Most mainstream laundry detergents (powder and liquid)
  • Laundry boosters and 'whitening' additives
  • Some fabric softeners and dryer sheets
  • Paper, envelopes, and packaging
  • A few skin-brightening cosmetics and some sunscreens

Alternatives

  • Detergents labelled 'no optical brighteners' or 'free & clear'
  • Oxygen bleach (sodium percarbonate) for actual whitening
  • Bluing agents — an older trick that washes out
  • Line-drying whites in sunlight, which does bleach them

Common questions

Are optical brighteners toxic?

Not in a meaningful way for humans at laundry exposures. The health concerns are allergy and photosensitivity in a small number of people. The stronger case against them is environmental persistence and aquatic toxicity.

Why do my clothes glow under a blacklight?

Because optical brighteners from your detergent are coating them. That's what they're designed to do — and why hunters and some outdoor users avoid brightener-treated clothing, since animals can see the UV glow.

Do brighteners make clothes cleaner?

No. They make fabric reflect more light so it appears whiter. Dirt and residue are still there. Detergents without brighteners may look less impressive on whites but clean just as well.

Can I remove optical brighteners from clothes?

Partially, over many washes with a brightener-free detergent. Some outdoor retailers sell strippers for this purpose, mainly for hunting clothing.
